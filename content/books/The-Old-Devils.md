I must admit I pored over this one for a good while, not really being able to get a grip on it for some time.

Kingsley Amis writes about a group of old friends and drinking buddies, their families and the drama between them. There's affairs - more than I thought - and punch-ups and deaths and everything you could want. The book faces what it is to be alive, to be old, and to die. Amis touches on legacy and history, the past and nostalgia.

Crucially one of the biggest themes touched on here is Wales, and what it is to be Welsh. Knowing a few modern Welsh people, there's a reclaimation of the land, language and history happening in the world. People are proud to be outspokenly Welsh, and to speak Welsh out. In this book from 1986, Amis writes of a more conservative Welshness, which finds that other kind to be showy and disingenuous. I don't recognise this version of Welshness - possibly this old guard has died out. Amis is also not shy of revealing a disdain for Dylan Thomas - though only in allegory, not by name. Whether that's fictional, for the purposes of the narrative, or belies Amis' own animosity, it's hard to say.

By the end I did enjoy this book. The drama is tantalising, the people feel real and normal and like I know them or am them, or could be them. And the way they view themselves - fat and old and immobile - and how they live and forgive one another, felt so so human. It's a real slice of life.
