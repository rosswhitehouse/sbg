I've written here before about my fanaticism around John Robins and Elis James. In fact just this week I finished the two-hundred-odd episodes of their old Radio X show, having previously completed their BBC Radio Five Live show and their co-written book, the Holy Vible.

This book was certainly something else entirely.

John Robins tells the story of his relationship with alcohol, writing through about the twelve drinks that changed his life. You'd be forgiven for thinking that the more serious, sombre topic of his sobriety and alcoholism would be no place for laughs, but I found the book to be witty and wry and silly too. Between the darkness - of which there is plenty - there are tangents about comedy and life and how stressful the process of writing a book. Robins changes tact with aplomb, and leaves this a darkly warm (warmly dark?) look back on his life to date.

This book very much made me consider my own relationship to alcohol. As John says, if we had the sort of research into alcohol that we do into cigarettes, it would almost certainly be banned. Now when I consider my own occasional drunken misstep, hangover anxiety or shame spiral, I think how it could so easily end up so much worse. Robins has come out okay so far, but there were many moments where the bottom didn't seem like it could get rockier, until it did. There but for a solid dash of good luck go I.

I believe this book came at the right time for me, it's filled with wisdom and poignance and sage advice and idioms. It's also hilarious to boot. It culminates in a sense of okay-ness that was sadly familiar to me. There have been good times and bad times, and there will be more of both to come. But we're okay right now. The feeling is not happiness, but a wary gratitude.

How does he do it?
